A few weeks after the ATAS website went live, messages started arriving. People from other countries wrote about partnerships. Students asked about internships. Someone offered to relocate to be part of it.

It was exciting. It was also a little frightening, because I understood something quickly: the website sounded bigger than the organization behind it.

## Why the website sounds confident

I write about ATAS with confidence because I believe in the problem and the direction. I am sure that Rwanda needs AI that understands its context, and I am sure about how I want to build it.

But confidence about direction is not the same as capacity. Believing in where you are going doesn't mean you have already arrived. ATAS is early. I am building it mostly on my own, and much of what I describe publicly is still work ahead.

The danger is not that the writing is confident. The danger is that a reader can't tell the difference between a bold direction and a finished reality.

## The rules I am setting for myself

**Describe the stage plainly.** If something is in development, say it is in development. If it is research, call it research. Status labels are small, but they are promises.

**Don't let anyone plan their life around something that doesn't exist.** This is the one I take most seriously. When a student offers to relocate for an internship, a vague yes could send them into a decision that costs them time and money. It is kinder to say clearly what exists now and what does not.

**Answer, even when the answer is "not yet."** Silence feels safer than admitting you are not ready, but it damages trust more than honesty does. A short message saying you are early-stage and will follow up costs almost nothing, and it keeps the door open.

**Say no to the right things.** Not every good opportunity is right at the right time. I would rather turn down a collaboration than accept something I can't deliver well.

**Audit your public claims regularly.** Read your own website as a stranger would. Check every number, every status, every address, every sentence that describes what you can do. Then fix anything that has run ahead of reality.

## Why this matters more for a young company

A young founder in a small country has one asset that is hard to rebuild: credibility. People remember when a claim turns out to be bigger than the truth, and they remember it for a long time.

The reverse is also true. Being honest about where you are makes everything you achieve afterward more believable.

## What I want ATAS to earn

I want people to read about ATAS and, months later, find that it did what it said. That kind of trust cannot be bought, and it is easy to lose in a single overstatement.

So the goal is simple. Keep the ambition, keep the honesty next to it, and let the work catch up to the words.
