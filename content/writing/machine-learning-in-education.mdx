Machine learning is transforming education in ways we couldn't have imagined a decade ago. From personalized learning paths to predictive analytics that identify struggling students early, AI is making education more effective and more accessible. But the transformation only works when it is grounded in the actual curriculum.

## Personalized Learning Paths

Every student learns differently. ML systems can analyze learning patterns and adapt content delivery to match individual needs — but personalization without a curriculum map is just noise. The question that matters is not "what does this student like?" but "what does this student know, relative to what they are expected to know?"

## Evidence, Not Just Grades

This is where our work on AcademiaPlus gets interesting. Because every question is mapped precisely to the Rwandan national curriculum, every graded response becomes evidence. Patterns surface across a class, a school, and eventually the country: which topics are genuinely hard, where teaching is landing, and what to focus on next.

## Predictive Analytics

Earlier predictive systems — including our EduBridge work on dropout risk — taught us an important lesson: schools act on signals they trust. Trust comes from transparency about how a prediction was made and what evidence supports it.

## The Impact

We've seen firsthand how these technologies improve learning outcomes and help teachers focus on what they do best — teaching. That is the standard every system we build has to meet: less administrative burden for teachers, more clarity for students, better decisions for schools.
