Language preservation in the digital age is crucial. Our Kinyarwanda Text-to-Speech project exists to bring our native language into modern AI applications on its own terms.

## Why This Matters

Many AI technologies are built for major languages, leaving smaller languages behind. The result is predictable: tools that mispronounce our names, misspell our places, and translate our ideas through someone else's worldview. We wanted to change that — starting with voice.

## Technical Challenges

Building a TTS model requires extensive data collection, phonetic analysis, and careful evaluation. For Kinyarwanda, there was no off-the-shelf foundation to lean on. Every step — dataset curation, phoneme coverage, naturalness evaluation — had to be built deliberately, with Rwandan speakers in the loop.

## Real-World Applications

From accessibility tools for visually impaired users to educational content creation, the applications are endless. Voice is the most natural interface there is — and it only works when it speaks your language well.

## Looking Ahead

This research feeds directly into ATAS's broader language program: speech recognition, natural language understanding that handles how Rwandans actually mix Kinyarwanda, English, and French, and techniques for building strong systems with limited, carefully collected data.

This is just the beginning.
