Starting ATAS (Alliance for Transformative AI Systems) has been one of the most challenging yet rewarding experiences of my life. At 20 years old, fresh out of high school, I decided to take a leap into AI entrepreneurship — not as a side project, but as a long-term commitment to building technology that understands Rwanda.

## The Vision

The idea was simple but ambitious: build AI systems that genuinely understand Rwanda — its languages, its geography, its culture, and the way Rwandans actually live, work, and communicate — rather than adapting tools built for other countries and hoping they translate.

We saw gaps everywhere: education systems drowning in manual work, voice technology that couldn't speak Kinyarwanda properly, and AI that treated our context as an afterthought. We knew these gaps were the work.

## Our First Product: AcademiaPlus

AcademiaPlus was born from a simple observation — teachers spend countless hours creating, digitizing, and grading assessments. What if the platform understood the curriculum itself?

Today it is built around three things rarely found together: the complete Rwandan national curriculum pre-loaded and shared by every school, a growing repository of real human-authored exam questions mapped precisely to that curriculum, and a grading engine that identifies not just whether an answer is right, but what it reveals about how the curriculum is actually being learned.

## Challenges We Faced

Building a company from scratch isn't easy. From technical challenges — building language systems for a language the major labs ignore — to business obstacles like pricing for schools and earning institutional trust, every day brought new lessons.

The biggest lesson so far: research excellence and practical delivery are not in tension. The rigor we apply to our Kinyarwanda language work is exactly what makes AcademiaPlus trustworthy in a classroom.

## Looking Forward

ATAS is just getting started. AcademiaPlus enters its first schools next academic term. IMIZI — our long-term contextual intelligence infrastructure — grows one deliberate layer at a time. And our language research keeps deepening the foundation underneath both.

The journey has taught me that age is just a number. Passion, dedication, and long-term commitment can carry an idea further than credentials ever will.
